Where my mind goes · Etched & the opportunity
Read me first

Hi Sumer, here's a little about how I'd approach my first 60 days at Etched, a team that sees itself as the Manhattan Project meets Bell Labs. As I see it, you're in a continued race for the inference market. Transformers have been validated since GPT-3, so this isn't new, but Etched has made a deliberately opinionated, architectural bet that transformer-specific inference will matter more over time than general-purpose compute like NVIDIA's. I can appreciate that bet.

I know a hire like this is a two-way door.

As Head of FDS (Forward Deployed Strategists) at Kindling, I lead a team of soon-to-be six strategists in a company of ~20. I work founder-close to understand how to authentically communicate their story. I'm a firm believer that if you're not building, you should be storytelling. I hold my team to a high standard, and our strategists should be able to partner with anyone, no matter how technical, to help them tell their story. So I understand how hiring like this works. Hire slow, and fire fast if it isn't quite right. We run the same principle internally. Below are a few thoughts on the upcoming timeline.

Ways of working · orchestration

Knowing which resources are required where

I sit inside the media team as an internal sounding board and QA. When Tom Duffy was writing our "Hiring the World's Best Editor" JD, he sent it my way for feedback before it went out.

The hire I'm proudest of is the editor who changed the trajectory of the media team. Everyone internally was hesitant because it was a high salary for a team that had been conservative to date, and the conversation had stalled. I knew the rooms we had access to demanded better editing quality, so I staked my job on it.

Tom can vouch that it changed our trajectory. That's honestly how I think about team building, knowing which resources are required where, and orchestrating around them with core creators.
